Kraków and Potsdam

Both columns come from the same dataset: Eurostat, Census 2021 population grid (1 km grid). The same age groups and the same rule: a cell belongs to the city if its centre lies inside the city boundary.

What we do not compare

A comparison only makes sense with the same definition. These indicators exist in both atlases but are computed differently:

  • Share under 18 from the Potsdam atlas: the grid for Kraków only gives the group under 15.
  • Number of buildings: BDOT10k in Poland and ALKIS in Germany define a building differently.
  • Construction period and heating: there are no open data for Kraków (the year of construction in the cadastre is paid, the central register of building emissions is not open).
  • Access to stops: the same frequency classification, but in Kraków measured from the centre of a 1 km cell, in Potsdam from a 100 m cell.
  • Districts and estates, e.g. Nowa Huta next to Drewitz in Potsdam: a 1 km cell is too coarse to compare districts, and Kraków's district boundaries come from OpenStreetMap.
  • Official census results for whole cities: in Poland from statistical areas, in Germany from the 100 m grid. The table shows both figures from the same 1 km grid.
